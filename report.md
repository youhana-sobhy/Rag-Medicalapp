# Annual Report 2025: Northbridge Health & Technology Group

This report summarizes the work of every division over the past year. It is a fictional document written for testing retrieval systems. Each section covers one division, and some sections reference the same incident or topic from different angles.

## Section 1: Executive Summary

Northbridge Health & Technology Group closed the year with steady growth across all divisions. The medical research team published four studies, the software engineering division shipped two major platform releases, and the cybersecurity team handled one significant incident, INC-2023-Q4-011, which carried over from the previous year's fourth quarter. Revenue grew modestly, and the company hired 85 new employees across offices.

## Section 2: Medical Research

The medical research division focused on cardiovascular health and sleep this year. One study followed 1,200 adult volunteers and recorded their resting heart rate each morning for six months. The normal adult resting heart rate is between 60 and 100 beats per minute. Well-trained athletes often have lower values, commonly between 40 and 60 beats per minute, and this is usually not a cause for concern. A resting rate that stays above 100 beats per minute, or drops below 60 together with dizziness or fainting, should be reviewed by a doctor.

The team also found that poor sleep, high caffeine intake and chronic stress were each linked to a higher morning resting heart rate. Participants who exercised moderately three times per week showed an average drop of five beats per minute over the study period. A "bug" in the wearable devices' firmware caused a small number of missing readings, which were excluded from the final analysis.

## Section 3: Software Engineering

The software engineering division released version 4.0 of the patient scheduling platform and version 2.5 of the internal analytics service. A major focus was reliability. Every service now sends a heartbeat signal every 30 seconds so the monitoring system can detect failures quickly. The team reduced the average deployment time from 45 minutes to 12 minutes by automating the testing pipeline.

During the third quarter, a configuration error in the scheduling platform caused a short outage. The same configuration weakness was later reviewed as part of incident INC-2023-Q4-011, and the engineering team added automated checks to stop similar errors from reaching production. Infection vectors in distributed systems, such as unpatched libraries, were also tracked and reduced by 60 percent.

## Section 4: Cybersecurity

The cybersecurity division responded to one major incident this year, INC-2023-Q4-011. The incident began when an employee account was compromised through a phishing email. The attacker gained access to a test server but did not reach any patient records. The security team detected unusual login activity within 40 minutes, disabled the account, and rotated all related credentials. A full review concluded that no personal data was exposed.

After the incident, the team introduced mandatory multi-factor authentication for all staff, expanded phishing awareness training, and shortened the log review cycle from weekly to daily. Two smaller events were also handled: a failed brute-force attempt against the VPN gateway and an expired security certificate on an internal tool.

## Section 5: Finance

Total revenue for the year was 48.2 million dollars, a 7 percent increase over the previous year. The fourth quarter was the strongest, with revenue of 14.1 million dollars, driven by new hospital contracts. Operating costs rose 4 percent, mainly because of hiring and cloud infrastructure. The finance team expects similar growth next year, with a stronger focus on controlling cloud spending.

Net profit margin improved from 11 percent to 13 percent. The company holds no long-term debt, and cash reserves are enough to cover about 14 months of operating costs.

## Section 6: Human Resources

The company grew from 410 to 495 employees during the year. Staff turnover fell to 8 percent, helped by a new flexible working policy and a mentoring program. Employee satisfaction surveys showed the highest scores for team collaboration and the lowest for internal communication, which the HR team plans to improve next year. A wellness program was introduced, offering free health screenings and subsidized gym memberships.

## Section 7: Customer Support

The customer support team handled 31,000 tickets this year, with an average first response time of 2.4 hours. Customer satisfaction stayed at 92 percent. The most common issues were appointment rescheduling, password resets and report downloads. After the release of version 4.0 of the scheduling platform, rescheduling tickets dropped by 35 percent. The team introduced a self-service help center that now resolves about one quarter of questions without a human agent.

## Section 8: Operations and Facilities

Operations opened a new regional office and moved two data connections to a faster provider. Energy use per employee fell by 12 percent after the installation of efficient lighting and smart thermostats. The team also completed a backup power test at the main data center, switching to generators in under 20 seconds with no service interruption.

## Section 9: Methodology

All figures in this report come from internal systems and were reviewed by the finance and compliance teams. Medical study results were reported as averages with outliers removed according to the rules set in the study protocol. Incident times were taken from system logs. Financial figures are unaudited and rounded to the nearest 0.1 million dollars. Where a section refers to an incident, it uses the incident's official identifier.

## Section 10: Outlook for Next Year

Next year the company plans to expand the medical research program to include blood pressure and activity data, release a mobile app for patients, and complete a full security audit. The leadership team has also approved a budget for training programs and for upgrading the oldest internal systems. The main risks identified are rising cloud costs, hiring competition and regulatory changes in the healthcare sector.
